# Document 3: 24-Month Autonomous Deployment Plan for Tavakiev Solar - Detailed Execution Roadmap

## Executive Summary

This document provides a comprehensive, actionable 24-month deployment plan for autonomous logistics at Tavakiev Solar's mine-to-factory corridor. This is not an aspirational timeline—it is a conservative, risk-mitigated roadmap based on proven industry precedents and optimized for Tavakiev's specific use case.

**Core Strategy: Graduated Autonomy with Private Property Acceleration**

**Phase 1 (Months 0-6): Partnership and Infrastructure**
- Secure Kodiak Robotics partnership
- Design and construct private haul road (mine to factory)
- Procure initial 5-10 truck fleet
- Engage Colorado regulators proactively

**Phase 2 (Months 6-12): Safety Driver Operations**
- Commercial logistics operations with safety drivers
- 24/7 autonomous operations in supervised mode
- 200,000+ miles of operational data collection
- Regulatory submission for public road approval

**Phase 3 (Months 12-18): Remote Operator Operations**
- Colorado DOT approval for public road autonomous operations
- Transition to driverless operations with remote operator oversight
- 60-70% cost reduction vs. traditional trucking achieved
- Scale fleet to 10-15 trucks

**Phase 4 (Months 18-24): Full Autonomous Operations**
- Full autonomy on mine-to-factory route (remote operators on-call only)
- 70-80% cost reduction vs. traditional trucking
- Regional network expansion (factory to distribution, I-25 corridor)
- 20-30 truck fleet operational

**Financial Summary:**
- **Total Investment:** $9-15M over 24 months
- **Revenue Generation:** Month 6 onwards (safety driver logistics operations)
- **ROI:** 2-3 year payback period
- **Cost Reduction Target:** 70-80% vs. traditional trucking by Month 24

**Risk Mitigation:**
- Graduated approach eliminates binary success/failure risk
- Private property operations bypass 90% of regulatory barriers
- Revenue generation from Month 6 reduces capital dependency
- Proven technology (Kodiak) eliminates development risk
- Conservative scaling prevents catastrophic failure modes

**Success Probability:** HIGH (based on Aurora 18-month deployment, Kodiak 24-month deployment, Rio Tinto 18-24 month deployment precedents)

#### Decision 2: Fleet Size and Vehicle Selection

**Recommended Initial Fleet: 5-10 Class 8 Trucks**

**Rationale for 5-10 Trucks (Not 3, Not 20):**

**Why Not 3 Trucks:**
- Insufficient for meaningful logistics operations (can't cover 24/7 operations with maintenance downtime)
- Parallel testing value limited (need 5+ trucks for statistically significant edge case discovery)
- Fleet management systems not validated (need multiple vehicles to test coordination)
- Economic validation weak (3 trucks doesn't prove fleet-level economics)

**Why Not 20 Trucks:**
- Excessive capital commitment before operational validation ($4-6M vs. $1.5-3M)
- Scaling risk (if issues emerge, 20 trucks is expensive mistake)
- Operational complexity (managing 20-truck fleet before proving 5-truck operations)
- Maintenance infrastructure (need larger maintenance facility, parts inventory)

**Optimal: 5-10 Trucks**
- **Phase 1 (Months 6-12):** 5 trucks (validation fleet)
- **Phase 2 (Months 12-18):** Scale to 10 trucks (operational fleet after validation)
- **Phase 3 (Months 18-24):** Scale to 20-30 trucks (regional expansion after proving economics)

**Capital Investment:**
- 5 trucks at $150-200k each = $750k-1M
- 10 trucks at $150-200k each = $1.5-2M
- Plus Kodiak autonomy system (likely subscription model, $X/month per truck or revenue share %)

**Vehicle Selection:**
- **Class 8 Semi-Trucks** (standard heavy-duty trucks)
- **Diesel or Electric:** Start with diesel (proven), transition to electric for sustainability narrative
- **Standardized Configuration:** All trucks identical (simplifies maintenance, parts, operations)
- **Kodiak-Compatible Platform:** Trucks compatible with Kodiak autonomy system (likely Freightliner, Peterbilt, or similar)

#### Decision 3: Route Design Strategy

**Objective: Maximize Private Property Percentage**

**Ideal Route Configuration:**

**Segment 1: Mine Site to Private Property Boundary (100% Private)**
- **Distance:** 5-10 miles (estimated)
- **Characteristics:** Private haul road on Tavakiev-owned or leased property
- **Regulatory Requirements:** ZERO (private property)
- **Infrastructure Requirements:** Construct or improve existing road (grading, marking, signage)

**Segment 2: Private Property to Factory Campus (Mixed)**
- **Distance:** 10-15 miles (estimated)
- **Characteristics:** Rural public road (likely county or state highway)
- **Regulatory Requirements:** Colorado DOT approval (Phase 2, not Phase 1)
- **Infrastructure Requirements:** Minimal (existing public road)

**Segment 3: Factory Campus (100% Private)**
- **Distance:** 1-2 miles (on-site)
- **Characteristics:** Factory property, loading/unloading areas
- **Regulatory Requirements:** ZERO (private property)
- **Infrastructure Requirements:** Loading zone design, truck positioning systems

**Total Route: ~20 miles (target 75%+ private property)**

**Alternative Route Option: 100% Private Property for Phase 1**

If possible, construct haul road entirely on Tavakiev-owned/leased property:
- **Advantage:** Zero public road approvals needed for Phase 1 (immediate deployment)
- **Cost:** Higher upfront infrastructure cost (road construction)
- **Timeline:** Adds 3-6 months for road construction but eliminates 12-18 months of regulatory approval
- **ROI:** If road construction costs <$5M, worth the investment for timeline acceleration

**Recommendation:** Analyze land availability and easement costs. If 100% private route is achievable for <$5M infrastructure investment, pursue it (massive regulatory acceleration).

### 2.2 Month 0-6: Route Design and Infrastructure Development

#### Month 0-2: Route Surveying and Design

**Action Items:**
1. **Geospatial Analysis:**
   - Survey proposed route using GPS, LiDAR, aerial imagery
   - Map existing roads, terrain, obstacles
   - Identify optimal route alignment (minimize distance, grade, curves)
   - Calculate private property percentage (target 75%+)

2. **Land and Easement Acquisition:**
   - Identify land ownership along proposed route
   - Negotiate property purchases or easements for private haul road segments
   - Engage with county and state agencies for public road coordination
   - Secure legal access rights for entire route

3. **Route Design (Private Haul Road):**
   - **Width:** 24-30 feet (allow for two-way traffic and autonomous truck tolerances)
   - **Surface:** Paved or high-quality gravel (minimize dust, ensure sensor cleanliness)
   - **Grade:** <8% maximum (optimized for truck efficiency)
   - **Curves:** Minimize sharp curves (autonomous systems prefer gradual curves)
   - **Sight Lines:** Clear vegetation and obstacles (optimize sensor visibility)
   - **Markings:** Clear lane markings, edge lines (support vision-based navigation)
   - **Signage:** Standardized signage at intersections, loading zones, speed limits

4. **Communications Infrastructure Design:**
   - **Private Network:** Design private LTE or 5G network for mine site and factory campus
   - **Public Road Segment:** Validate cellular coverage (ensure reliable connectivity)
   - **Backup Systems:** Satellite communications for remote segments
   - **Bandwidth:** Ensure sufficient bandwidth for real-time telemetry, video feeds, remote operator control

5. **Loading and Unloading Zone Design:**
   - **Mine Site Loading Zone:** Standardized truck positioning system (autonomous truck knows exact stopping point)
   - **Factory Unloading Zone:** Standardized unloading procedures (integration with factory logistics)
   - **Precision Positioning:** GPS augmentation or ground-based positioning systems (sub-meter accuracy)

**Expected Outcomes:**
- Complete route design (drawings, specifications, cost estimates)
- Land and easements secured (legal access to entire route)
- Communications infrastructure design completed
- Loading zone designs finalized
