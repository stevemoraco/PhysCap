**Day 14: Wiley Rein Prevailing Wage Compliance Program Delivery**

**Deliverable**: Wiley Rein delivers draft prevailing wage & apprenticeship compliance program

**Background**: To qualify for the full 5x credit multiplier under §45X (and other IRA credits), Tavakiev must satisfy prevailing wage and apprenticeship requirements. These requirements are based on the Davis-Bacon Act and are complex.

**Program Components**:
1. **Prevailing Wage Compliance**:
   - Identify all "covered" construction and alteration activities (facility retrofit, equipment installation)
   - Determine applicable Davis-Bacon wage rates for Colorado Springs (by trade classification)
   - Implement certified payroll reporting for all contractors and subcontractors
   - Establish weekly wage verification and recordkeeping

2. **Apprenticeship Compliance**:
   - Calculate required apprentice-to-journeyman ratios (typically 12.5% to 15% of labor hours)
   - Partner with registered apprenticeship programs (Colorado Building Trades, IBEW Local 113)
   - Implement apprenticeship hour tracking and reporting
   - Establish good-faith compliance procedures

3. **Contractor Oversight**:
   - Make prevailing wage and apprenticeship compliance contractual requirements for all construction contracts (EPC, equipment installers)
   - Establish pre-qualification process for contractors (verify prior Davis-Bacon compliance)
   - Implement weekly site audits during construction (certified payroll review, apprentice interviews)

**Strategic Importance**: Failure to comply with prevailing wage requirements results in reduction of §45X credit from 5x to 1x (e.g., module credit drops from $0.07/W to $0.014/W). This is an $112 million annual difference at 2 GW production. Compliance is non-negotiable.

**MES §45X Traceability Module Requirements**:

   - Yield tracking (scrap rates, rework, defects)

   - Daily production reports automatically generated

   - Automated import of test results (module performance, cell efficiency, materials verification)
   - Linkage of test results to serial numbers (which modules were tested)

6. **Audit Trail & Recordkeeping**:
   - All data immutable after entry (no retroactive changes without audit log)
   - Electronic signatures for manual data entry (operator ID, timestamp)
   - Automated daily backup to secure cloud storage (AWS GovCloud for ITAR compliance)

   - Automated calculation of eligible credits based on production data and cost accounting

## Part VI: Contingency Planning - When Policy Goes Wrong

**Trigger**: IRS audits Tavakiev's first §45X claim and disallows credits due to substantiation deficiency.

**Potential Causes**:
- Incomplete serial-level traceability (gaps in MES data)
- Insufficient cost accounting documentation (can't prove direct production costs)
- FEOC certification defects (supplier certifications deemed unreliable)
- Independent testing inadequate (NREL partnership not formalized)

**Financial Impact**: Loss of $50-70M annual credit revenue; must service operating costs and debt from product sales alone.

**Mitigation (Pre-Audit)**:
- **Action 1**: Wiley Rein conducts annual internal compliance audit before IRS filing (catch deficiencies early)
- **Action 2**: Engage Big Four accounting firm (Deloitte, PwC) to provide independent opinion on substantiation adequacy
- **Action 3**: Request IRS pre-filing conference (informal guidance on substantiation approach)

**Response (Post-Disallowance)**:
- **Immediate**: Wiley Rein files administrative appeal with IRS Appeals Office
- **30 Days**: Conduct internal investigation to identify substantiation gap (MES data, cost accounting, FEOC certs)
- **60 Days**: Remediate gap for future claims (MES upgrade, cost accounting process tightening, supplier re-certification)
- **6-12 Months**: If appeal unsuccessful, consider litigation (U.S. Tax Court) - this is expensive ($1-2M legal fees) and uncertain

**Contingency Capital**: Maintain $20-30M working capital facility to cover 6-12 month period without credit monetization.

**Mitigation (Pre-Application)**:

- **Action 2**: Secure multiple committed offtake agreements (Microsoft, Google, Meta) to demonstrate bankability
- **Action 3**: Emphasize Base Case unit economics (profitable without §45X) to address margin concerns
- **Action 4**: Position humanoid robotics as upside (not critical path) to de-risk technology perception

**Response (Post-Rejection)**:

- **30 Days**: If concerns are addressable (e.g., need more offtake certainty), revise and re-submit application

### 6.3 Scenario 3: FEOC Guidance Retroactively Disqualifies Suppliers

**Mitigation (Pre-Guidance Change)**:

- **Action 2**: Conduct annual third-party audits of supplier FEOC certifications (don't rely solely on supplier attestations)

- **30 Days**: Obtain FEOC certifications from replacement suppliers
- **60 Days**: Resume §45X credit claims with updated supplier documentation

**Contingency Inventory**: Maintain 90-120 days of inventory for critical components (polysilicon, wafers, cells) to buffer against supply chain disruptions during FEOC remediation.

### 6.4 Scenario 4: Congressional Repeal or Amendment of IRA

- **30 Days**: Revisit vertical integration timeline (delay polysilicon/wafer phases if credits are insufficient to justify CapEx)
- **6-12 Months**: Increase pricing to hyperscaler customers (pass through lost credit revenue as higher module ASP)

  - **Vertical Integration Margin**: Capture full supply chain margin (polysilicon → module) provides cost advantage even without credits
