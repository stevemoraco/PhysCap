**2. Risk Transfer: Pass-Through Pricing vs. Fixed-Price Exposure**

**Meyer Burger:**
- Signed fixed-price supply agreements (DESRI 5 GW contract at undisclosed price)
- When Chinese panel prices crashed to $0.088/W, Meyer Burger was contractually locked into pricing that required §45X credits to break even
- Customer terminated contract when Meyer Burger couldn't deliver (Nov 2024)
- **Result:** 100% of policy risk and 100% of price risk borne by Meyer Burger

**Tavakiev Solar:**
- **Pass-through pricing structure** in customer contracts:

  **Price Formula:**
  ```
  Panel Price = $0.15/W (base cost) + 50% of realized §45X credit value
  ```

**Scenario A: Full Credits (§45X at $0.07/W module credit)**
- Customer pays: $0.15 + (50% × $0.07) = **$0.185/W**
- Tavakiev receives: $0.185 ASP + $0.07 credit = **$0.255/W total**
- Customer still pays **34% less than Chinese imports** ($0.28/W), even after sharing upside

**Scenario B: Credits Cut 50% (to $0.035/W)**
- Customer pays: $0.15 + (50% × $0.035) = **$0.1675/W**
- Tavakiev receives: $0.1675 ASP + $0.035 credit = **$0.2025/W total**
- Tavakiev maintains margin; customer shares policy risk but still wins on price

**Why This Matters:**
- **Risk distribution:** Customer participates in credit upside, but shares downside. Meyer Burger bore 100% of downside alone.
- **Customer incentive alignment:** Customers (hyperscale data centers, DOD) benefit from domestic content ITC bonus (10% adder worth $150M+ on a 2.5 GW project), so they have economic reason to accept pass-through pricing.
- **Bankability:** Lenders can underwrite minimum base price ($0.15/W) even in worst-case credit scenario, making project financeable. Meyer Burger's fixed-price model became unfinanceable when policy risk rose.
